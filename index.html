Lenny Lupin-Jimenez

Hi, I'm Lenny. I'm a PhD student in Applied Mathematics at UC Santa Cruz, building physically consistent deep learning emulators for complex, time-dependent systems. My work spans turbulence and ocean–atmosphere modeling, generative data assimilation, and equation discovery. I also apply these pipelines to other complex system modeling to recapture physically consistent behavior, in ways that maintain partial mechanistic interpretability.

Outside of research, I play volleyball, hike, practice sitar, and listen to new music.

Ongoing · UC Santa Cruz

Full-field reconstruction and equation discovery in nonlinear systems

Recovering a full spatiotemporal field from a time-series signal measured at a single point in cryogenic soliton propagation, and using the recovered field to identify higher-order terms in the governing PDE.

Ongoing · UC Santa Cruz

Spectral theory of diffusion-based reconstruction for turbulent flows

How noise schedules and turbulence energy profiles govern the scale-dependent accuracy of diffusion generative models, across both large- and small-scale features.

Ongoing · Los Alamos National Laboratory

Multimodal kilonova detection

A live pipeline for identifying kilonova events in survey telescope data, fusing multimodal streams to catch rare transients as they happen.

2025 · UC Santa Cruz

Generative data assimilation under extreme sparsity

Reconstructing full ocean and turbulence states from satellite and Lagrangian observations at up to 99.9% sparsity, by conditioning diffusion models on neural operator outputs.

2024–2025 · Fujitsu Research of America

Physically consistent regional ocean emulation

Autoregressive emulation of Gulf of Mexico sea-surface dynamics, where a spectral loss holds decade-long rollouts stable, with a generative model downscaling to 4 km in the same step.
