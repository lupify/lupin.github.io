Research

2023 – present Santa Cruz, CA

Deep learning emulation of nonlinear geophysical systems

Theoretical & Applied Complex Systems Lab, UC Santa Cruz — PhD researcher, advised by Ashesh Chattopadhyay

Autoregressive stability, spectral accuracy, and generative data assimilation for emulators of turbulence and the ocean–atmosphere system. Ongoing work recovers full spatiotemporal fields from a single-point time series in cryogenic soliton propagation, using them to identify higher-order terms in the underlying PDE, and asks how noise schedules and turbulence energy profiles govern the scale-dependent accuracy of diffusion-based reconstruction.

  • Generative Lagrangian data assimilation for ocean dynamics under extreme sparsity N. Asefi, L. Lupin-Jimenez, T. Wu, R. He, A. Chattopadhyay Machine Learning: Earth 1, 015009 2025 arXiv:2507.06479
Ongoing Los Alamos, NM

Los Alamos National Laboratory — Research intern

2024 – 2025 Santa Clara, CA

Physically consistent regional ocean emulation

Fujitsu Research of America, Converging Technologies Lab — AI geophysical researcher

Autoregressive emulation of Gulf of Mexico sea-surface dynamics, where a spectral loss objective keeps rollouts stable and physically consistent at 8 km resolution over decadal timescales without unphysical drift. A physics-constrained generative model coupled to the emulator downscales and bias-corrects to 4 km in the same step. Also contributed physics-informed neural operator wave forecasting and sparse data assimilation to SWR-Viz.

  • Simultaneous emulation and downscaling with physically consistent deep learning-based regional ocean emulators L. Lupin-Jimenez, M. Darman, S. Hazarika, T. Wu, M. Gray, R. He, A. Wong, A. Chattopadhyay JGR: Machine Learning & Computation 2(3) 2025 arXiv:2501.05058
  • SWR-Viz: AI-assisted interactive visual analytics framework for ship weather routing S. Hazarika, L. Lupin-Jimenez, R. Vuppala, A. Chattopadhyay, H. Y. Wong Advances in Visual Computing (ISVC 2025) Springer 2026 arXiv:2511.15182

Machine learning for tokamak disruption avoidance

Princeton Plasma Physics Laboratory — DOE SULI fellow, with Egemen Kolemen

Real-time prediction of plasma disruptions and tearing modes in magnetic confinement fusion. Ensemble models trained on several thousand DIII-D discharges were adapted to run inside the real-time Plasma Control System, steering shots away from instability while holding performance high.
